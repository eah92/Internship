Model & assumptions

z₁ + p₁/ρg + V₁²/2g = z₂ + p₂/ρg + V₂²/2g + h_turbine + h_loss   ⟶   H_gross = h_turbine + h_loss
  • A run-of-river bulb turbine: the headwater and tailwater are the river up- and downstream of the barrage, both at atmospheric pressure. The gross head is the difference between the two river levels (typically low — a few metres to a few tens of metres). Design flow here is ≈ 9 000 cusecs (≈ 255 m³/s).
  • The scheme is set by positioning the water passage — the intake sill, the bulb (turbine) centreline and the draft-tube outlet — relative to the two fixed river levels; the riverbed is fixed while the impounded headwater deepens with head.
  • Head loss h_loss = (f·L/D + ΣK)·V²/2g through the intake and draft-tube passage; Darcy friction factor f from the Swamee–Jain correlation (laminar 64/Re below Re = 2300), iterated with the flow. The pressurised conduit is treated with Darcy–Weisbach (not open-channel Manning).
  • The runner + wicket gate act as a variable nozzle: Q = C_d·A_throat·(gate)·√(2g·H_net), so closing the gate throttles the flow and re-partitions the head. Shaft power P = η·ρg·Q·H_net.
  • Cavitation is flagged when the minimum absolute pressure (usually the runner exit / draft tube) reaches the water's vapour pressure; a low Thoma σ is also warned — bulb turbines are set below tailwater for margin. Backflow / no-flow is flagged when the tailwater is at or above the headwater.
  • This is a steady-state teaching model — it does not resolve surge/water-hammer transients, unsteady runner dynamics, or real turbine efficiency curves. Values are indicative.
